SELECTED PROJECTGenerative AI · Personal prototype

A physics tutor with textbook context.

A tutoring prototype for Current Electricity that brings textbook passages and relevant past conversations into a language model's context. It explores how retrieval, conversation recall, and clarification can support a more focused educational chat experience.

Upload a textbook→Retrieve context→Ask & discuss
Explore the approach ↗

Retrieval and conversation recall

PDF text is extracted, split into chunks, and stored in a ChromaDB collection. For each question, the prototype retrieves relevant passages and looks up related exchanges in a separate conversation-memory collection. Recent chat history and retrieved context are assembled into the prompt for generation.

Handling questions that need more context

The code rewrites follow-up questions using recent conversation history. It also includes embedding-based checks for educational relevance, a clarification path for ambiguous short questions, and NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails integration to steer the conversation toward the intended subject.

A small interface for experimentation

A Gradio interface supports PDF upload, chat, and controls to clear or export conversation memory. This is a personal prototype for exploring retrieval-augmented generation and conversational design; the source includes the implementation and configuration.

EARLIER PROJECTApplied ML · Around 2021

Making sense of invoice payment patterns.

An earlier notebook-based workflow for predicting payment delay from historical invoice data. It follows the problem from cleaning dates and encoding customer information to comparing regression models, then turns predictions for outstanding invoices into estimated payment dates and delay bands.

9 models compared6 payment-delay bands
Explore the approach ↗

Defining the prediction problem

Payment delay is defined as the difference between an invoice's clearing date and due date. Settled invoices provide the target, while invoices without a clearing date are set aside for prediction. The notebook sorts records by posting date before creating the initial training, validation, and test subsets.

Preparing features and comparing models

The workflow standardizes date fields, removes duplicate and uninformative columns, encodes customer and payment categories with an unknown-category fallback, and derives the time between posting and the due date. It compares nine regressors, including linear models, decision trees, gradient boosting, and XGBoost, and includes code for a five-fold XGBoost parameter search.

Turning predictions into usable outputs

Predicted delays are grouped into six bands and added to due dates to estimate when outstanding invoices may be paid. This is an exploratory modeling project. The later notebook scores follow refitting on the same evaluation subsets, so I do not present them as independent test performance.

LEARNING EXERCISESDeep-ML & MakeMore

Building intuition for how models learn.

Learning implementations in NumPy and PyTorch, working through forward and backward passes, gradient checking, and training loops. The exercises span general neural-network components, character-level generation, and convolutional building blocks.

Explore the approach ↗

Following gradients through a network

The NumPy neural-network exercise implements finite-difference gradient checks, dense and activation layers, loss functions, sequential composition, and mini-batch training. Explicit forward and backward functions make the flow of values and gradients visible.

Exploring a different model structure

In a MakeMore notebook, custom PyTorch layers form a WaveNet-inspired hierarchy over an eight-character context. The work includes embeddings, batch normalization, training, and character-by-character sampling, with notes on how normalization changes for three-dimensional activations.

Convolutional building blocks, in progress

The NumPy CNN exercise includes convolution, max-pooling, ReLU, and dense-layer forward and backward functions. The full network assembly and end-to-end training portions are still in progress in the public repository.
